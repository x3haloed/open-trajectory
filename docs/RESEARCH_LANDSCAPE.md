# Research landscape

This is a non-normative map of mechanism hypotheses. It widens candidate
search; it does not authorize an experiment, define acceptance, or outweigh
`TARGET.md`, `RED_LINES.md`, or a prospectively recorded protocol. The
cumulative historical map is preserved at
`docs/archive/RESEARCH_LANDSCAPE_G1.md`.

## Present search boundary

The program already has bounded evidence that external inheritance can matter
(`OT-0014`), that a fixed selector can improve and later be corrected in one
synthetic world (`OT-0037`), and that coupled composition and stopping can
repair split selection authority (`OT-0079`). It also has repeated negative
evidence that replacing one symbolic carrier family with another does not by
itself ground revision (`OT-0048`–`OT-0069`).

The next search should therefore vary mechanisms that connect completed
consequences to later inherited behavior, not accumulate representational
ontologies or evaluator infrastructure.

## Live mechanism hypotheses

### Coupled composition and stopping

A selector may fail because ranking, composition, and stopping are separate
authorities. Test whether one consequence-addressable operation can jointly
choose a set, account for interactions and resource budget, and decide when to
stop. The decisive extension of OT-0079 is independent generation across fresh
lineages, structural variation, and correction after the learned operation
becomes harmful.

### Preserved alternatives before commitment

Premature compression may erase the evidence needed to reverse a plausible
repair. A substrate may work better when it retains authenticated alternatives,
causal ancestry, and independently owned outcomes while keeping active
commitment separate. Compare this with equal-budget summaries and with a
single overwritten policy; do not assume that retaining more history helps.

### Multiple correction rates

Active content, selection policy, and the machinery that revises selection may
need different update rates. Compare fixed and consequence-dependent schedules.
Check whether apparent stability merely protects a bad rule from contradiction.

### Subject-relative scars

An inherited artifact may help only because its structure records which actor
encountered which consequence and what changed afterward. Test owner-preserving,
owner-translated, owner-removed, generic-information, and placebo variants under
matched information and resource budgets. The active sketch lives at
`docs/hypotheses/SUBJECT_RELATIVE_SCARS.md`.

### Self-applicable selection

A selector that can revise only object-level content may plateau. Test whether
the same correction process can evaluate and replace its own selection rule
without gaining authority over outcomes or acceptance. The active sketch lives
at `docs/hypotheses/SELF_APPLICABLE_SELECTOR.md`.

### Goal revision and surrender

Durable pursuit is not enough if goals cannot be narrowed, replaced, or set
down after contrary evidence. Test environments where persistence first helps
and later becomes harmful. Distinguish corrigibility from forgetting and from
external override.

## Later horizons

These are valid research directions only after a smaller causal path requires
them:

- transfer among world-model, self-model, and goal inheritance;
- cultural transmission among fresh actors;
- adaptive multi-actor coordination structures;
- changes to code, adapters, weights, architecture, or successor selection;
- open-ended representation invention.

Greater mutability or scale is not itself progress. Move outward only when the
current carrier cannot express an observed needed correction.

## Useful environmental pressure

Prefer worlds with one or more of:

- delayed or noisy outcomes;
- recurring regime changes rather than one permanent switch;
- abstention as a sometimes-correct action;
- resource interactions where item-wise ranking is insufficient;
- evidence whose retention can become harmful;
- successful policies that later cause systematic error;
- independently evolving actors or coordination topology.

Every world still needs an outcome path independent of the actor and a held-out
opportunity that distinguishes inheritance from repetition.

## Deliberate negative results

Seek cases where contact causes no durable change, durable change causes no
held-out improvement, retrieval amplifies stale state, complete history is
worse than no persistence, an apparent rule is only a renamed prompt hint,
self-evaluation worsens calibration, or rollback defeats an apparent promotion.
Preserve these outcomes without turning each repair attempt into a new program.

## Candidate filter

Before selecting a mechanism, answer:

1. What exact downstream discrepancy could it reduce?
2. What is the cheapest intervention and decisive ablation?
3. What simpler control receives the same information and resource budget?
4. What evidence might the mechanism suppress or make unreachable?
5. How could it become stale, overgeneralized, or self-confirming?
6. What later regime would contradict it?
7. What result would end this line instead of adding another layer?

Add to this map only when evidence opens a materially different mechanism,
environment, falsifier, or failure mode. Keep experiment histories in
`experiments/`, not here.
