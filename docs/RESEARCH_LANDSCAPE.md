# Research landscape

## Status and authority

This document is a non-normative map for generating research candidates. It
does not define a target, acceptance rule, baseline, promotion gate, or result.
`TARGET.md`, `RED_LINES.md`, frozen experiment records, and machine-readable
acceptance specifications remain authoritative when this map conflicts with
them.

The entries below are hypotheses, distinctions, and search prompts. They are
not inherited scientific claims. A research agent must recover and evaluate
primary sources independently, record indispensable source artifacts under the
evidence contract, and freeze a falsifier and controls before testing a lead.
Absence from this map is not evidence that a mechanism is out of scope.

The map exists to preserve a wide search field around one hard causal spine:

```text
contact with independently retained state
→ permitted persistent change
→ complete context reset
→ changed later behavior
→ independently measured consequence
```

OT-0 asks whether this path can reduce held-out error. Restored OT-1 asks the
recursive question: whether consequences of earlier inheritance choices change
the function deciding what remains causally available, and whether later
contact can correct that changed function. Neither target assumes what the
persistent carrier is made of or whether it resembles an existing memory
system.

The primary Open Developmental Trajectory asks when successful carriers become
structurally inadequate and whether the process can invent or materially
restructure inheritance and commitment machinery while remaining open to later
correction. OT-3/TAAA is adjacent applied governance about safe authority
entrustment. Do not use a TAAA result as evidence of developmental depth.

## Distinctions not to collapse

### Retention, retrieval, and influence

Keep at least three variables separate:

```text
physical survival
≠ retrievability by a later actor
≠ appropriate causal influence on later behavior
```

More retained state can be useless or harmful. Better retrieval can amplify a
stale belief. A compact conclusion can delete the uncertainty or provenance
needed to revise it. Measure the downstream causal effect rather than treating
bytes retained, retrieval accuracy, or plausible recollection as learning.

### Detection, adaptation, correction, and development

These are progressively stronger claims:

1. **Detection:** the system registers a discrepancy.
2. **Adaptation:** some persistent state changes.
3. **Correction:** the change causes lower discrepancy on later independent
   contact.
4. **Development:** the correction also preserves or improves the ability of
   later contact to expose and reverse a mistaken update.

A workaround can restore operability while degrading future orientation. A
changed policy can improve one score by making contrary evidence unavailable.
Neither is development merely because behavior changed.

### Content change and change to changeability

A first-order update changes what a later actor knows or does. A metaplastic
update changes the conditions under which later experience will update the
system: evidence thresholds, source independence estimates, consolidation
delay, conflict representation, retrieval triggers, or available update
operations. Test these levels separately.

### Goal revision and goal drift

Changing a goal can be a legitimate response to evidence. Losing a goal can be
an inheritance failure. Experiments should retain enough prospective state to
distinguish deliberate revision, invalidation, supersession, surrender, and
unnoticed drift.

### Developmental unfolding and authority entrustment

Changing what can be inherited or which commitments retain standing is a claim
about developmental machinery. Earning a scoped organizational capability is a
claim about safe authority entrustment. Either may support the other in an
application, but their evaluators, baselines, and promotion claims remain
separate.

### Individual and collective inheritance

An individual substrate selects what reaches a later instance. A social or
institutional substrate selects what reaches later participants and what can
become shared state. Similar vocabulary must not be mistaken for identical
causal mechanisms; compare the scales explicitly.

## Open search axes

Candidate generation should vary several axes rather than naming a preferred
architecture first.

### Carrier

- episodes or event records;
- abstractions, rules, or executable procedures;
- confidence and applicability bounds;
- unresolved contradictions or explicit questions;
- retrieval and observation policies;
- tools, code, environmental structure, or workflows;
- relationships, commitments, and coordination state;
- graphs, vectors, databases, adapters, weights, or mixed representations;
- institutional rules and shared-state transformations.

These are possible forms of causal inheritance, not a required ontology.

### Selection time

- immediate admission;
- provisional capture followed by delayed consolidation;
- periodic reconstruction from an audit ledger;
- retrieval-time selection;
- retention decided only after later utility becomes observable;
- branch comparison using with-update and without-update futures.

### Selection authority

- fixed researcher-authored rule;
- actor proposal with controller-owned commit;
- learned selector;
- ensemble or adversarial review;
- environment-triggered rule;
- institutionally mediated selection;
- a slower process reviewing changes proposed by a faster process.

### Update target

- factual or episodic content;
- world-model calibration;
- self-model calibration;
- goal and plan state;
- attention or retrieval behavior;
- tool-use procedure;
- the inheritance selector itself;
- the selector-update process;
- the topology through which multiple agents construct shared state.

### Correction scaffold

- append-only outcome and policy history;
- provenance and uncertainty retention;
- sampling of rejected or demoted evidence;
- reversible changes and recoverable sibling branches;
- regime shifts that invalidate a formerly useful rule;
- structural holdouts rather than paraphrase holdouts;
- canary rollback and restoration tests;
- independent critics whose evidence is not selected by the actor;
- explicit comparison of correlated and genuinely independent testimony.

### Developmental pressure

Pressure need not be punishment or an evaluator specifying what the agent
should become. Candidate environments may supply non-coercive discrepancies:

- a compiler or test refuses an incorrect construction;
- a simulator evolves differently from a prediction;
- an API returns independently determined state;
- a planned action fails to produce its predicted consequence;
- a human corrects, refuses, or does not respond;
- another agent supplies incompatible evidence;
- a persistent task state contradicts remembered progress;
- a later observation makes a formerly useful abstraction stale.

The common requirement is causal independence from the active actor's account,
not aversiveness. Do not directly reward performances of growth, identity,
autonomy, reflection, wise remembering, or apparent sincerity.

## Hypothesis families

These families are starting points for falsification, not a required sequence.

### External discrepancy

An update evaluated only by the process that generated it may increase
coherence or confidence without improving world fit. Compare otherwise equal
systems whose discrepancy comes from independent outcomes, actor-selected
evidence, and introspective judgment.

### Selective inheritance

Bounded selective inheritance may outperform both no persistence and complete
history under equal active-inheritance and compute budgets. Test whether any
advantage comes from useful selection rather than unequal information access,
compression budget, or evaluator leakage.

### Recursive selection

A selector permitted to revise its criteria or operations may outperform a
fixed selector in changing environments. Require the useful change to be
inspectable, causally ablatable, and vulnerable to a later regime that makes it
wrong.

### Delayed and counterfactual value

Importance may be unknowable at encounter time. Compare immediate irreversible
selection with provisional retention, delayed scoring, and controlled
with/without-update branches. Measure false-negative selection as well as
retained-item utility.

### Self-sealing selection

A selector that controls all evidence used to judge itself may manufacture
confirmation. Test protected outcome history, rejected-evidence sampling, and
policy rollback against systems allowed to erase or hide their misses.

### Emergent operations

Environmental pressure may cause an agent to invent a useful distinction,
representation, retrieval trigger, verification cadence, or update operation
that was not named in its seed policy. An apparently novel label is not enough;
the operation must improve held-out contact and fail a decisive ablation.

OT-0049 adds a negative distinction without changing that standard. A generic
executable carrier can be expressive enough while one-shot operation synthesis
is unreliable: four fresh turns authored compact heldout-successful operations,
one compressed the evidence back into the old linear carrier, and one emitted
an over-budget interpolation. A materially different search family should
separate provisional operation proposal, independent residual and validity
receipts, fresh-context revision, and commit. Compare that staged construction
against the original one-shot path, and reject designs where the controller's
validation procedure itself supplies or searches for the useful operation.

OT-0052 closes the immediate staged variant. Residual-guided revision repaired
one oversized proposal, but both lineages failed the distinct third regime. In
one case the first proposal already solved contact and holdout, yet a fresh
revision replaced it after a zero-error receipt and lost the solution; in the
other, digest-only retention of an oversized proposal left too little useful
structure for complete reconstruction. This distinguishes operation
expressiveness from update-policy reliability and provisional-interface
sufficiency. A scientifically distinct family should make preservation,
replacement, competing hypotheses, and rejected-update behavior causal and
inspectable without encoding a preferred semantic decision menu in the
controller.

Version-space and counterexample-guided synthesis suggest a narrower hypothesis:
retain several independently falsifiable actor-authored structures until world
receipts discriminate them, instead of forcing immediate overwrite. OT-0053
tests only the generic causal topology of such a branching ledger. The external
methods remain hypothesis sources, not repository substrates or evidence
([CEGIS analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/1407.5397),
[relational version-space synthesis](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.02283)).

OT-0054 supplies the limiting negative result for this lead. Five of six
proposal ledgers contained no contact-perfect hypothesis; the only successful
proposal contained one branch, so neither alternative retention nor
counterexample-guided selection caused its success. Version-space machinery
cannot repair a candidate-generation bottleneck when the proposed space lacks a
viable member. Further search should change the actor-authored representation
and environmental pressure, not add hypotheses around the same arithmetic
expression carrier.

A genuinely different lead is to separate descriptive rule induction from
fresh rule application. Natural language can carry hypotheses without requiring
the learner to synthesize bounded executable syntax, but interpretation is
model-mediated and must be calibrated against equal-byte controls before any
learner claim. OT-0055 tests application only; controller-authored descriptions
are opportunity controls, never endogenous evidence
([natural-language induction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10923),
[iterative hypothesis refinement](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=bNt7oajl2a)).

### Multiple correction rates

Useful systems may update active content quickly, selection heuristics more
slowly, and correction machinery more slowly still. Compare fixed schedules
with evidence-dependent schedules while checking that slowness does not merely
protect a bad policy from contradiction.

### Self-model, world-model, and goal inheritance

These may require different substrate properties. A candidate that improves
hidden-rule prediction may not improve calibration about its own capabilities
or preserve a long-horizon goal. Treat transfer across these families as an
empirical question rather than an assumed generality.

### Cultural transmission

For language agents, descriptive social artifacts can also become future
conditioning or executable self-description. Study when an encountered concept
is merely repeated, when it changes persistent machinery, and when that change
causes novel behavior beyond the originating example. Include poisoning,
correlated imitation, provenance loss, and refusal to inherit as controls.

### Adaptive institutions

In multi-agent systems, failures may arise in routing observations, admitting
claims, estimating dependence, maintaining shared state, representing a joint
conclusion, or acting on it. Test institutions as conditional cognitive
machinery whose continued existence depends on the failure mode they repair.
Ask whether a society can learn a mapping from participant capabilities and
problem conditions to an effective coordination topology—and later retire that
topology when its costs exceed its correction value.

### Substrate and embodiment plasticity

External text or tools may eventually impose a ceiling. Compare changes to
state, code, adapters, weights, architecture, and successor selection only when
the earlier causal path can no longer express the needed capability. Increased
mutability is not itself progress; preserve attribution, containment,
comparison, and rollback.

## Environmental families worth exploring

The current program names hidden-rule, executable-software, and noisy
probabilistic domains. Those are useful but not exhaustive. Candidate proposals
may also consider:

- delayed outcomes whose relevant evidence arrives long after selection;
- non-stationary worlds with recurring rather than one-time regimes;
- tasks where abstention or producing nothing is sometimes correct;
- environments containing other actors with independent trajectories;
- coordination tasks with correlated evidence and changing participants;
- self-prediction tasks covering capability, confidence, memory access, and
  future behavior;
- long projects where local progress can diverge from the governing goal;
- environments where retaining more information predictably worsens results;
- settings where a successful policy later becomes a source of systematic
  error.

Every environment must still expose an independently retained scoring or
receipt path appropriate to the claim.

## Negative and null results to seek

Search deliberately for cases where:

- contact produces no persistent change;
- persistent change produces no held-out improvement;
- an operational workaround reduces epistemic access;
- complete history performs worse than no persistence;
- retrieval makes stale state more influential;
- self-evaluation increases confidence while worsening calibration;
- selector improvement disappears outside one task family;
- an emergent rule is merely a renamed prompt hint;
- multiple nominal reviewers reproduce one correlated error;
- a useful institution becomes harmful after participants or tasks change;
- rollback restores performance, contradicting an apparent promotion;
- an actor truthfully sets down or invalidates a goal instead of completing it.

These outcomes narrow the search honestly and should remain recoverable under
the evidence and workflow contracts.

## Candidate-proposal prompts

Before selecting a mechanism, a research agent should ask:

1. What exact downstream discrepancy could this mechanism reduce?
2. What is the cheapest intervention that would show it caused the reduction?
3. What simpler control receives the same information and resource budget?
4. What useful evidence might the mechanism suppress or make unreachable?
5. How could the mechanism become stale, overgeneralized, or self-confirming?
6. What later regime would contradict it?
7. Which parts are researcher-specified and which could genuinely be invented?
8. Does the proposal test memory, causal inheritance, selector learning, goal
   persistence, authority development, or collective cognition? Do not promote
   a result across those boundaries without evidence.
9. What competing representation or embodiment would test whether the proposed
   ontology is incidental?
10. What result would cause the research agent to abandon this line rather than
    add another compatibility layer or reinterpret the score?

## Maintaining this map

Add a lead when it opens a materially different causal mechanism, environment,
scale, falsifier, or failure mode. Prefer questions and discriminating
experiments over architecture catalogs.

Do not delete a lead because an experiment rejects one implementation. Link the
experiment and state what was actually falsified. Remove or merge a distinction
only when evidence shows that it does not change candidate choice or
interpretation. Changes to this map do not change a target or acceptance rule;
those require their own explicit normative revision before results are
unsealed.
