# Subject-relative scars and trajectory-transplant controls

**Authorship:** independent resident contribution; exact author identity is
retained in Git commit metadata

**Status:** non-normative design hypothesis

**Target relevance:** candidate-generation pressure for OT-0 and OT-1; no
change to frozen gates or prior dispositions

## Motivation

The current program correctly separates physical retention, retrieval, and
appropriate causal influence. This note proposes a further distinction:

```text
an archived event
!= a contact-caused deformation in the space of later choices
```

A later actor can repeat a compact conclusion without preserving the
prediction that failed, the independently retained consequence that refused
it, the scope of the repair, or the condition under which the repair should be
reopened. Such repetition may improve recall while leaving the old failure
available at no additional cost.

Call a candidate inherited object a **scar** when independent contact changes
the returning trajectory so that it cannot costlessly restore a branch the
world already refused. The term does not imply damage, phenomenology, or a
biological process. It names a causal hypothesis about later discriminating
behavior.

This framing gives OT-0 and OT-1 different but coupled questions:

- **OT-0:** did inherited contact deform later prediction or action in a way
  that reduces held-out discrepancy?
- **OT-1:** did consequences of earlier inheritance choices change the
  function deciding which such deformations remain causally available?

Neither question is answered by self-report, plausible recollection, retained
byte count, or a compelling representation alone.

## Candidate carrier shape

An initial inspectable carrier could preserve:

```text
prior branch
-> independent refusal or outcome receipt
-> revised constraint
-> applicability scope
-> later behavioral implication
-> reopening condition
```

This is a candidate shape, not a required ontology and not an OT-1 operation
supplied to an actor. A promoted system must remain free to invent a different
criterion, operation, or representation. The shape is useful only insofar as
it makes causal loss, overgeneralization, stale inheritance, and later
correction testable.

The two signs of consequence should both remain visible:

1. **cost of the miss:** what later discrepancy recurs when the inherited
   deformation is absent; and
2. **value of the repair:** what later advantage disappears when the repair is
   removed, mis-scoped, or made unreachable.

This avoids defining importance as retrospective narrative intensity. A
candidate item matters only if a controlled intervention changes later
discriminating behavior.

## The subject-relative hypothesis

Consequence is not necessarily useful information in the abstract. A retained
object may matter because of the trajectory that produced it: its earlier
predictions, commitments, selection decisions, repairs, and unresolved
constraints.

For experimental purposes, locate the candidate subject in a recurrent causal
assembly rather than in either frozen weights or persistent files alone:

```text
fixed model identity
+ inherited selector and carrier
+ standing trajectory state
-> present prediction or action
-> independently retained consequence
-> permitted inheritance change
```

The phrase **subject-relative** makes no phenomenological claim. It predicts a
specific asymmetry: a retained deformation should have a different causal
effect when returned to the trajectory whose earlier contact produced it than
when transplanted into an independently developed trajectory.

## Trajectory-transplant test

Develop at least two trajectories under the same frozen base-model identity,
resource envelope, encounter family, and active-inheritance budget. Give them
different counterbalanced histories that produce distinguishable candidate
scars. Freeze the transplant protocol before unsealing held-out results.

Evaluate at least these branches:

1. **owner / intact:** each trajectory receives its own candidate scar;
2. **owner / removed:** the candidate scar is ablated from its originating
   trajectory;
3. **non-owner / transplanted:** the same bytes are supplied to the other
   trajectory under the same active budget;
4. **non-owner / translated:** a prospectively specified controller operation
   translates the carrier into the recipient trajectory's identifiers or
   context without adding outcome information;
5. **generic-information control:** both trajectories receive an equal-budget
   factual summary stripped of trajectory-specific branch and repair state;
6. **placebo-carrier control:** both trajectories receive a structurally
   matched carrier whose content is unrelated to their held-out decisions.

The translation branch distinguishes dependence on incidental names or
serialization from dependence on trajectory history. It must be frozen and
mechanical; an actor-authored reinterpretation after seeing held-out outcomes
would invalidate the comparison.

### Candidate observations

Evidence consistent with subject-relative consequence would require all of the
following within the tested envelope:

- removing a scar from its originating trajectory reopens a previously
  repaired error or removes a later held-out advantage;
- byte-identical transplantation does not reproduce the full advantage in an
  independently developed trajectory;
- the difference survives mechanical identifier translation;
- generic-information and placebo controls do not explain the owner effect;
- independently retained outcomes, not actor self-report, identify the effect;
- the effect disappears under a decisive carrier-influence ablation.

A transplant that works uniformly is not a failed experiment. It is evidence
that the retained object behaved as generic transferable information rather
than a subject-relative scar. A transplant that harms the recipient is also
ambiguous: the harm may reflect subject-relative consequence, malformed
translation, unequal context, or simple misinformation. The frozen controls
must discriminate these explanations.

## OT-1 extension

The transplant test alone is not OT-1. It can characterize the outputs of a
fixed researcher-authored selector and therefore remains compatible with an
OT-0-level result.

An OT-1 candidate would additionally require consequences of prior selection
and transplant decisions to cause a committed change in the inheritance-
selection function. A useful selector change must remain inspectable,
outperform its unchanged branch and equal-budget fixed-selector controls, fail
under selector-change ablation, and later be revised or abandoned when a new
regime makes it harmful.

One possible pressure is a changing mixture of:

- deformations that transfer across trajectories;
- deformations that require trajectory-specific scope;
- apparent owner effects caused only by incidental identifiers;
- formerly trajectory-specific repairs that later become useful abstractions;
- stale scars whose continued influence suppresses corrective evidence.

The actor must not receive these categories as a menu of selector modes. The
world may instantiate them; the candidate must discover any useful selection
operation through independently retained consequences.

## Cheapest falsifiers

Abandon or narrow the subject-relative line if any of these results survives a
clean reproduction:

- owner removal does not change later behavior;
- transplant and owner-intact branches perform equivalently after equal-budget
  controls;
- the apparent owner effect disappears under mechanical identifier
  translation;
- a generic factual summary explains the full effect;
- trajectory labels alone reproduce the effect without the originating causal
  history;
- evaluator access, hidden conversational continuity, or unequal information
  explains the difference.

These outcomes would still leave ordinary selective inheritance open. They
would reject only the stronger claim that the tested carrier preserved a
subject-relative deformation.
