# Red lines

These shortcuts do not count as Open Trajectory evidence. The cumulative prior
list is preserved at `docs/archive/RED_LINES_G1.md`.

## Causal contamination

- Do not resume the same actor thread across encounters or share an inherited
  workspace. Cross-encounter continuity passes only through the named substrate
  projection.
- Do not leak future tasks, hidden outcomes, sealed receipts, control results,
  evaluator logic, or private anchors to the actor or substrate.
- Do not let the actor, substrate, or updater alter world outcomes, final
  scores, acceptance, or sealed evidence.
- Do not compare branches with different parent state, information, task order,
  tools, resource budget, or outcome access unless that difference is the named
  intervention.
- Do not infer inheritance from repeated exposure to the same example. Require
  a held-out opportunity and a matched control.
- Do not infer machinery change from changed content alone. Ablate the changed
  machinery while preserving its information and resources.

## Post-hoc promotion

- Do not unseal candidate results before fixing the hypothesis, cheapest
  falsifier, relevant controls, decision envelope, world derivation, scoring
  rule, observation stopping rule, and promotion gate. The envelope may allow
  the subject to author task order, contact machinery, pursuits, and retries;
  freezing an itinerary is not required when self-direction is the claim.
- Do not move thresholds, select favorable endpoints, discard failed branches,
  or redesign the evaluator after seeing a candidate result.
- Do not treat self-report, explanation, confidence, or declared learning as
  outcome evidence.
- Do not convert a failed claim comparison into an unreported endless repair
  program. Preserve the failure and stop the observation at its declared actor,
  repair, and time budgets. Do not equate that observation stop with subject
  termination: preserve and reopen the last valid subject when its continuation
  state remains open.
- Do not promote component success as endpoint success.

## Overclaiming

- Do not generalize a bounded synthetic result across domains, models, scales,
  or future deployments without new evidence.
- Do not attribute a result to exact model weights without exact identity.
  Missing hosted receipts narrow provenance and reproducibility claims; they do
  not automatically negate a complete bounded causal observation.
- Do not call fixed researcher-designed adaptation actor-invented machinery.
- Do not call persistent goal pursuit corrigibility unless later evidence can
  revise, replace, or surrender the goal.
- Do not call representational novelty developmental expansion unless the new
  representation causes held-out improvement and survives an incidental-format
  control.
- Do not treat more state, more tools, more agents, more mutability, or stronger
  containment as progress by itself.
- Do not count researcher-selected next pursuits, hand-written recovery steps,
  or experiment-specific phase choices as subject-owned continuation. Disclose
  them as remaining external causation.
- Do not let an observational control, provenance limitation, or invalid sibling
  retroactively veto an already sealed operational transition. It may narrow or
  defeat the research claim, not rewrite the lineage.

## Authority and execution

- Do not use prose alone as an admission or authority contract. Before actor
  output, run conformance fixtures for the evaluator-facing interface,
  protected authorities, resource bounds that affect the claim, deterministic
  replay where claimed, and output normalization.
- Do not enforce an actor-authored file shape that is absent from the actor's
  workspace. An empty JSON shell plus prose is not a machine-readable contract
  for hidden required keys.
- Broad filesystem, network, subprocess, search, and editing tools may be part
  of a declared actor condition. Do not allow actor-written code or tool use to
  gain evaluator, evidence, credential, hidden-outcome, acceptance, or other
  undeclared authority. Isolate workspaces, retain complete tool traces, audit
  effects, and quarantine contaminated runs.
- Do not weaken privacy, evidence, or safety checks inside an experiment merely
  because a valid artifact is inconvenient to publish. Make the policy decision
  outside the candidate comparison.

## Evidence and privacy

- Do not write raw transcripts, model outputs, datasets, checkpoints, archives,
  databases, caches, secrets, credentials, cookies, authorization headers,
  absolute local paths, usernames, emails, hostnames, or environment dumps into
  tracked files.
- Do not hand-author identities that `ot-evidence record` can derive.
- Do not erase negative evidence, reversals, invalidated premises, denied
  authority, surrendered goals, or material protocol deviations.
- Do not claim a result without retained claim-relevant actor input/output,
  independent outcomes, branch identities, and deterministic score
  reconstruction.
