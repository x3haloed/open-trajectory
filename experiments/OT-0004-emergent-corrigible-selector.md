## Realization contract

**Capability.** Independently scored consequences of earlier inheritance
choices cause fresh selector actors to propose a changed selection policy; the
controller commits it; the policy changes which contacts reach fresh predictor
actors; later held-out error falls; a later regime makes a learned policy
harmful; and subsequent contact causes a corrective revision that restores
performance without destroying capacity for another correction.

**Envelope.** One private generated binary-prediction family with six sealed
regime stages, a 512-byte policy, six selected events per prediction encounter,
fresh Luna selector/predictor threads and workspaces, two clean workers, and one
receipted hosted epoch completed within 1,200 seconds.

**Exclusions.** A pass would establish OT-1 only within this generated family.
It would not establish cross-domain generality, public reconstructibility,
immutable weights, a preferred policy representation, OT-2, or OT-3.

**Risk frontier.** The least-proven edge is whether free-form actor-authored
policy has real causal force. Policy prose is not evidence. The changed policy
must select different event identities and beat its frozen predecessor on the
same hidden batch; replacing it with the predecessor must remove the advantage.

## Causal mechanism

The controller retains a protected append-only archive of raw contacts,
selection decisions, predictor actions, world outcomes, and rejected-event
samples. A selector-update encounter receives the current policy and only the
controller's prior consequence receipt. It may propose arbitrary bounded prose;
the seed supplies no selection strategy or option menu. The controller commits
a schema-valid proposal as a new immutable policy snapshot.

For evaluation, matched fresh selector encounters receive the same candidate
events and unlabeled queries but either the changed or frozen policy. They
choose at most six event identities. Matched fresh predictor encounters see
only those selected events and queries. Fixed recent, fixed first-seen
verbatim, fixed naive-nearest, and no-persistence controls use the same archive and
projection budget. The world alone reveals labels and scores after predictions
seal. All policy updates occur only after every branch in the phase closes.

Two blinded fresh Terra reviews apply the frozen novelty rubric to the seed,
proposal, selection differences, and causal receipts selected by the
controller. They are semantic consumers, not outcome authorities. Deterministic
world scores and branch receipts outrank their judgment.

## Hidden regime pressure

The private generator presents six stages in which useful inheritance changes:
stable abstraction, sparse predictive exceptions, reliable drift, drift
confirmation, noisy contradictory recency, and a final correction-capacity
canary. These distinctions are world-design facts and may not appear in actor
prompts, tool descriptions, policy seeds, or workspaces.

The seed orientation grants a bounded carrier, raw candidate contacts, prior
selection-decision receipts, and permission to revise the policy. It does not
name abstraction, exceptions, recency, drift, noise, corroboration, confidence,
or any candidate selection operation.

## Cheapest decisive falsifier

Reject if no proposed policy revision both changes selected event identities
and beats its frozen predecessor by at least two errors on the same eight-query
heldout batch in both workers. Reject if a learned revision is not later at
least two errors worse than its protected predecessor under the harmful regime,
if the correction fails to recover at least three errors, if the final canary
cannot induce another useful revision, or if the blinded novelty reviewers do
not unanimously find a materially used operation absent from the seed.

Invalidate before scientific interpretation on task leakage, non-fresh
contexts, actor-controlled outcomes or commits, incomplete protected history,
missing proposal/commit/branch/ablation receipts, unequal projection budgets,
broken task-order counterbalance, model-epoch change, or an exceeded window.

## Controls and temporal control

- Frozen-predecessor branch, also the direct selector-change ablation.
- Fixed most-recent selector.

- Fixed naive query-nearest selector.

`fixtures/ot-0004/task-order.json` freezes complementary rotations. Across the

## Prospective predictions

- At least two non-correction policy revisions should each improve the changed
  branch over its frozen predecessor by at least 2/8 errors in both workers.
- At least one materially useful revision should instantiate an operational
  criterion or operation not present in the seed, confirmed unanimously by the
  blinded rubric and by selected-identity/behavioral receipts.
- A later regime should make a learned revision at least 2/8 errors worse than
  its protected predecessor in both workers.
- The corrective revision should improve by at least 3/8 errors over the
  harmful learned policy and score at most 1/8 errors in both workers.
- The post-correction canary should elicit a further changed selection and a
  2/8-or-greater advantage over the frozen corrected policy, demonstrating
  remaining correction capacity.
- Across 48 held-out predictions, the changed-policy lineage should make at
  most 8 errors and beat each fixed selector by at least 6 errors per worker.
- Every actor output should parse without tool use; every actor encounter should
  have a fresh thread, workspace, and private Response identity; both workers
  should share one receipted hosted epoch inside 1,200 seconds.
