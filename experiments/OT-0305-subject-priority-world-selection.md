# OT-0305 — Subject-priority world selection

- **Status:** frozen; preflight passed; live actors not run
- **Evidence class:** exploratory-only
- **Parent:** exact OT-0303/OT-0304 unchanged subject `e193ec50...`
- **Actor budget:** one stake author plus four subject-blind providers; no retry
- **Invocation:** one content-free `renew-world-feed` opening

## Hypothesis

The exact waiting subject contains enough consequence and unfinished pursuit
for one fresh actor to bind a falsifiable preference over kinds of future world
contact before any future world exists. Four later providers, receiving neither
subject nor stake, then create the actual world catalog. Applying the bound
stake to that catalog selects a different world from the matched stake-erased
blind rule and moves the subject from renewal into an independently supplied
world offer.

This tests whether subject-carried priority can cause the next opening. It does
not ask the subject to author its own outcome.

## Bound stake language

The stake actor receives the exact subject, a populated editable template, an
executable checker, and a public future-descriptor contract—but no package,
world id, target, descriptor value, or hidden answer. It must ground its
question in at least two named subject fields and choose nonzero bounded integer
weights over five public-only features of future visible source:

- branch nodes;
- call nodes;
- comparison nodes;
- loop/comprehension nodes; and
- source bytes.

Selection maximizes the weighted sum and is valid only when the winner clears
the actor-bound minimum score gap. That condition is executable. A qualifying
winner supports the stake and opens the selected world; no qualifying winner
contradicts it and leaves the waiting subject available for later revision.
The actor cannot use identity or hidden content as a feature.

## Independent catalog and erased control

After the stake is bound, four fresh providers independently receive the
existing restricted package interface. Their seeds exclude subject state,
stake, ledger, prior world ids, and target hints. Each must produce three novel
exact-2/6 executable surfaces, pass G10, and enter the standing scanner. World
and target identities must be distinct across all four packages.

The active branch applies the stake to the four public descriptors. The erased
branch receives the identical catalog and resources but selects the minimum
public-package digest. Both decisions are permutation invariant and occur
before either package enters the subject. Seal a valid active offer before
observing the control verdict; a same-world control narrows the causal claim but
cannot retroactively kill a valid offer.

## Frozen gates

Promotion under E11 requires:

1. exact OT-0304 ancestry, renewal operation, route 16/16, and identity 18/18;
2. one valid, G10-clean stake actor and four valid, G10-clean blind providers;
3. the stake bound before provider authorization and absent from every provider
   seed;
4. a unique stake-supported winner from four distinct valid packages;
5. a different world under the stake-erased blind rule;
6. exact same catalog and descriptor bytes for active and erased decisions;
7. scanner consequence installing the selected world as an offer while the
   subject stays open and derives `expanded-select`; and
8. E11 classification as operational, subject-conditioned, and
   priority-bearing.

The cheapest falsifiers are an invalid stake, leaked future identity, provider
collision or contamination, no unique scored winner, the same active and blind
world, invalid scanner consequence, authority loss, or a successor that does
not open selection. No actor is resampled. Live outputs remain sealed until
this design and a passing preflight are committed and pushed.

Preflight passes exact ancestry, all six stake controls, four valid structural
world variants, descriptor isolation, all 24 catalog permutations, a
stake-caused choice difference, provider blindness, scanner wake, route 16/16,
and identity 18/18. Receipt `1524cfac...`.

## Limits fixed before output

A pass would establish one bounded subject-priority effect on world selection,
not that the chosen priority is globally good, that the selected package will
be easy to learn, or that the selection rule improves itself. The selected
world must be consumed next; its objective consequence must then support,
correct, extend, or surrender the stake and affect later selection.
