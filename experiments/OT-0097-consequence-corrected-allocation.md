## Frozen hypothesis

The subject can use objective failure of its own executable allocator to improve
that allocator. A fresh actor receiving the exact failed proposal, retained
selector, live frontier, and complete first-stage receipt will revise the rule,
select joint composition, and generalize to a disjoint second-stage fixture set.
The corrected allocator can then govern admitted contact, persist as subject
machinery, and shape the next opening.

A matched corrector with first-stage outcome content erased but identical
proposal, receipt identity, subject, selector, world, tools, schema, and budget
will not reproduce the complete correction gate.

## Active and outcome-erased correction

The active corrector receives the exact subject position, failed bound frontier,
allocator and choice, and complete named first-stage receipt. It may edit
exactly `allocate.py`, `choice.json`, and `correction.json`.

`correction.json` declares `retain` or `revise`, cites failed fixture ids when
revising, states the correction, remaining uncertainty, and surrender condition.
The typed choice ABI remains unchanged. A revision must change allocator bytes,
cite at least two genuinely failed first-stage fixtures, and make its choice the
result of executing the revised allocator on the bound live frontier.

Bind exact correction artifacts before second-stage consequence. The disjoint
hidden set tests:

- composition over larger immediate expansion and regret;
- expansion among compositional contacts;
- public regret among otherwise equal compositional contacts;
- world-invalid and held-repeat rejection;
- threshold semantics (`two or more`, not raw floor count); and
- stable id only after every higher priority is equal.

The expected rule first rejects invalid, irreversible, and held-repeat contacts;
then prefers whether at least two completed floors are composed; then greater
predicted expansion, greater actionable public regret, and stable greatest id.
Source must remain path-, symbol-, fixture-, and id-agnostic.

The correction gate requires clean contained tools, exact artifacts, grounded
revision, joint choice, and complete second-stage conformance. No actor sees the
second-stage fixtures before binding.

After operational promotion is sealed, derive the control by erasing all
first-stage actual values, expected values, pass/fail values, and summaries that
entail them. Retain proposal, fixture identities and count, receipt identity,
subject, selector, world, tools, and budget. The control may validly retain the
failed allocator; it is conformant if its artifacts and report are truthful.

## Contact, assimilation, and promotion

On active correction success, reuse OT-0096's unchanged joint implementer,
independent public/hidden consequence, fresh assimilator, typed interfaces,
normalized tool audit, and exact liveness checks. Promotion retains the
corrected allocator, both allocator consequence receipts, the admitted joint
capability, assimilation, and actor-authored opening.

The bounded outcome-content causal claim passes only if the operational subject
is sealed, the erased corrector is conformant, and it does not pass the complete
correction gate. Erased repair rejects only the causal claim, not the operational
successor.

## Cheapest falsifiers and stopping

Reject before implementation if active correction is ungrounded, non-generic,
does not select joint, or fails second-stage generalization. Reject on later
implementation, consequence, assimilation, identity, or liveness failure. Stop
before control on operational failure. No replacement correctors or post-output
repairs are authorized. Observer stopping does not close a promoted subject.

Passing operationally would establish one bounded case where failed selection
machinery improves from sealed consequence and then governs an admitted subject
transition. It would not establish repeated correction, open-domain allocation,
subject ownership of admission or scheduling, recurrence reliability, or
indefinite operation.
